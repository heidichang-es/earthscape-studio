以前做陶是一件輕鬆愉快的事。一團土從開始盤起，到釉燒完成出窯，中間可以拖上半年——想到就做一點，沒想到就放著。乾燥從來不是一個需要煩惱的問題。

這次不一樣。作品尺寸往上堆疊之後，大型花器一放進窯裡，兩個就把空間佔滿了。以前一窯塞十幾個小東西，容錯率很高，炸一個還有十幾個陪著（而且其實從沒炸過）；現在一窯兩件，炸一個就是一半。

## § 01 · 炸坯

每次開窯都像是驚喜包，不知道今天是全員通過還是全軍覆沒。

不是釉燒階段的問題，是素燒——也就是說，它們進窯的時候還帶著水分。我以為乾了。摸起來是乾的，顏色看起來也均勻，但那只是表面的狀態。

沒有乾透的坯，就算打開時完整無損，但可能變得異常脆弱，一碰就碎。

## § 02 · 重新學晾坯

過去那套「放著就好」對小件有效，對大件則還要煩惱口緣與底部厚薄不一的收縮崩裂。這次補的功課還有一件：**用塑膠布控制乾燥速度**。

把坯包起來，讓水分慢慢走，而不是任它在空氣裡自己快乾。表面比內部乾得快，收縮就不一致，除了乾燥時有可能會出現裂縫，應力留在土裡，等到素燒升溫的時候還會再結算一次。包上塑膠布，等於把「快乾」這個變數拿掉。

以前不需要這一步，是因為拖延症已經替我做完了慢乾。現在尺寸放大、一窯只有兩個位置，就得自己把那半年還回來。

> 以前是時間替我完成晾坯。現在得自己思考實踐。

## § 03 · 倖存的那個

炸掉的沒有拍照，當時還沒購入相機。也希望沒有下次。

For years, making pots was an easy, unhurried pleasure. A lump of clay could take half a year to travel from the first coil to a finished glaze firing — a little work when I thought of it, and otherwise left alone. Drying was never something I had to worry about.

This time is different. Once the work started stacking upward, two large vessels were enough to fill the kiln. A kiln packed with a dozen small things is forgiving — lose one and twelve remain (and in truth I had never lost one). A kiln with two pieces in it is not: lose one and you have lost half.

### § 01 · Blown bisques

Every kiln opening is a lucky bag. I never know whether today is everyone through, or nobody at all.

Not a glaze-firing problem — a bisque problem, which means they went in still carrying water. I thought they were dry. They felt dry, the colour looked even. But that was only the surface.

And a piece that hasn't dried through can come out whole and still be strangely fragile — it breaks at a touch.

### § 02 · Learning to dry, again

*Leave it alone and it'll be fine* works for small work. With large work there is also the uneven thickness between rim and base to worry about, and the shrinkage that tears a piece apart along that difference. So there was one more lesson to go back and learn: **use plastic sheeting to control the rate of drying.**

Wrap the piece so the water leaves slowly, instead of letting it race off into the air. The surface dries faster than the interior and the shrinkage goes uneven — cracks can open during drying itself, and the stress left in the clay gets settled a second time when the bisque firing climbs. Sheeting the piece takes *fast* out of the equation.

I never needed this step before because procrastination had already done the slow drying for me. Now that the work is bigger and the kiln only has two places in it, I have to pay that half-year back myself.

> Time used to do the drying for me. Now I have to think it through and do it myself.

### § 03 · The one that survived

The photograph is one of the pieces that came through, taken as it went into the kiln for its bisque firing. It has since been fired and is out of the kiln.

I didn't photograph the ones that blew — I hadn't bought a camera yet. And I hope there won't be a next time.
